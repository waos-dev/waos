# How frameworks composite native surfaces, and how they give a compositor root no window shows

Research for two Inset design questions, 2026-10-30. Nothing under `/Users/mac/code` was changed.

1. Inset's `RenderSurfaceView` (`crates/inset-rendering/src/surface_view.rs`, `origin/main`) paints its rectangle with `BlendMode::Clear` and separately records the placement through `SceneBuilder::add_surface` (`crates/inset-embedder/src/scene_builder.rs`), which records the rectangle and clip, not the hole. Every surface sits beneath the view's one GPU-drawn buffer. PR #11 (`draw-only-what-paints`, `crates/inset-embedder-winit/src/rendered_contents.rs`, `has_rendered_contents`) has to infer "this frame paints only holes" by walking the recorded ops for upright cleared rectangles. The lead chose "a clear hole in the framework's picture, surfaces beneath" (`2026-10-29-media.md`, decision 3; `2026-10-29-media-research.md` §1).
2. Inset's PR #12 (`detached-surface-root`) adds `MacOs::detached_root() -> (Arc<dyn SurfaceParent>, Retained<CALayer>)` on macOS: surfaces opened under it go into a `CALayer` the app places itself; WaOS puts it in a private `CAContext` that a window process hosts with `CALayerHost`.

Every claim is marked:

- **[read]**: read in source code or documentation, cited;
- **[inferred]**: my conclusion from what was read, not checked by running anything.

Sources read: local checkouts of Flutter (`/Users/mac/code/flutter`, 2026-08-24, engine under `engine/src/flutter`), Chromium (`/Users/mac/code/chromium`, 2026-08-15), WebKit (`/Users/mac/code/WebKit`, 2026-09-24) and microsoft-ui-xaml (`/Users/mac/code/microsoft-ui-xaml`, 2026-09-04); AOSP `frameworks/base` main from android.googlesource.com; GTK main from gitlab.gnome.org and its GitHub mirror; androidx and JetBrains/compose-multiplatform-core (`jb-main`) and flutter/packages from GitHub; Microsoft and Qt documentation. Paths below are relative to those roots.

## 1. Compositing native surfaces with the framework's drawing

### What each one records, and who makes room

### Flutter

- **Framework.** `PlatformViewLayer.addToScene` calls `builder.addPlatformView(viewId, offset: rect.topLeft, width:, height:)` and nothing else; `supportsRasterization()` is false **[read: `packages/flutter/lib/src/rendering/layer.dart`, `PlatformViewLayer`]**. The render object paints no hole **[read: same]**.
- **Engine.** `PlatformViewLayer::Preroll` hands the transform and mutator stack (clips, opacity) to `ExternalViewEmbedder::PrerollCompositeEmbeddedView`. `PlatformViewLayer::Paint` does `context.canvas = view_embedder->CompositeEmbeddedView(view_id_)`, so every later layer paints into the slice above that platform view **[read: `flow/layers/platform_view_layer.cc`]**. Who makes room is the engine, from paint order; the framework only says where in the order the platform view is **[inferred]**.
- **Embedder API.** A frame reaches the embedder as an ordered list of `FlutterLayer`s, each either `kFlutterLayerContentTypeBackingStore` (a buffer Flutter rendered) or `kFlutterLayerContentTypePlatformView` (with its mutations), each with offset and size; a backing store carries `FlutterBackingStorePresentInfo.paint_region`, "the area of the backing store that contains Flutter contents. Pixels outside of this area are transparent" **[read: `shell/platform/embedder/embedder.h`, `FlutterLayer`, `FlutterBackingStorePresentInfo`, `FlutterCompositor`]**. Backing stores come from the embedder's `create_backing_store_callback`, and `present_view_callback` receives the list per view **[read: same]**.
- **Dropping empty slices.** `EmbedderExternalView::HasEngineRenderedContents` dispatches the slice into a `DlOpSpy` and answers `did_draw() && !slice->is_empty()` **[read: `shell/platform/embedder/embedder_external_view.cc`]**. `DlOpSpy` counts a draw only when its paint is not transparent or it draws an image **[read: `shell/common/dl_op_spy.{h,cc}`, `setColor`, `drawColor`]**. `LayerBuilder::AddExternalView` adds a slice's Flutter contents only if it has rendered contents; `PrepareBackingStore` makes a render target only for layers that have Flutter contents; `PushLayers` pushes platform-view layers and then the layer's backing store, if any **[read: `shell/platform/embedder/embedder_external_view_embedder.cc`]**. So a frame whose only content is platform views produces no backing store at all **[inferred from that code]**. `LayerBuilder` also merges slices into as few layers as give the same picture: a platform view goes into the deepest layer whose contents it does not intersect, contents into the first layer from the back they intersect (`GetLayerForPlatformView`, `GetLayerForFlutterContentsRegion`, "https://flutter.dev/go/optimized-platform-view-layers") **[read]**.
- **macOS.** The embedder shows the first backing store as a `CALayer` whose contents are its IOSurface, and each later one only in the rectangles of its paint region, as sublayers with a `contentsRect` **[read: `shell/platform/darwin/macos/framework/Source/FlutterSurfaceManager.mm`, `UpdateContentSubLayers`, `presentSurfaces`]**. Platform views are `NSView`s in a `FlutterMutatorView` whose `layer.zPosition` is the layer's index; the paint regions of backing stores above a platform view become hit-test-ignore regions **[read: `FlutterCompositor.mm`, `PresentPlatformViews`]**.
- **iOS.** The `FlutterView`'s own layer is the background; platform views are its subviews, above it **[read: `FlutterPlatformViewsController.mm`, `addSubview`]**. `SliceViews` intersects each slice's draw region (its r-tree) with the platform views beneath it; where they meet, it allots an overlay (`FlutterOverlayView`, its own Metal layer above that platform view), clips that region out of the background with a difference clip, and renders the rest of the slice into the background **[read: `flow/view_slicer.cc`, `SliceViews`; `FlutterPlatformViewsController.mm`]**. Intersection rects are unioned into one per platform view "so it doesn't grow forever" **[read: `view_slicer.cc`]**.
- **Android.** Four modes **[read: `docs/platforms/android/Android-Platform-Views.md`]**: Virtual Display (the view rendered into a `VirtualDisplay` feeding a Flutter `Texture`); Hybrid Composition (the real `View` in the hierarchy, "the Flutter widget tree is divided into two different Android `View`s, one below the platform view and one above it", composed on the platform thread); Texture Layer Hybrid Composition (the view placed in the hierarchy but its `draw` redirected into a Flutter `Texture`; a `SurfaceView` inside it "will be drawn at the wrong location and/or z-index"); HC++ (API 34, Impeller, Vulkan; opt-in). HC++ keeps a single overlay surface above all platform views and fakes per-view stacking with difference clips for platform views that come later in the order **[read: `shell/platform/android/external_view_embedder/external_view_embedder_2.cc`]**; its documented limit: a transparent platform view does not show correctly in "Flutter canvas → Platform View → Overlay → Transparent Platform View" when all four intersect **[read: the same doc]**.
- **Video in practice.** `video_player` offers `VideoViewType.textureView` and `.platformView` **[read: `video_player_platform_interface.dart`]**; on Apple the texture path copies pixel buffers (`TextureBasedVideoPlayer: FlutterTexture`) and keeps an invisible `AVPlayerLayer` to work around blank encrypted streams **[read: `video_player_avfoundation/.../TextureBasedVideoPlayer.swift`]**.

### Chromium (viz)

- **What the content records.** The renderer's compositor emits a video frame as a texture quad flagged `is_video_frame`, with a `protected_video_type` **[read: `components/viz/service/display/ca_layer_overlay.cc`, `PutForcedOverlayContentIntoUnderlays`]**. Chromecast emits a `VideoHoleDrawQuad`, used "to instruct that a video overlay is to be activated", carrying the plane's id **[read: `components/viz/common/quads/video_hole_draw_quad.h`]**. Nobody upstream paints a hole **[inferred]**.
- **Who decides.** The display compositor, every frame, trying strategies on the root pass's quads **[read: `overlay_processor_using_strategy.cc`]**. The underlay strategy "looks for a video quad without regard to quads above it. The video is 'underlaid' through a black transparent quad substituted for the video quad"; the candidate gets `plane_z_order = -1`, and the primary plane is re-checked with `is_opaque = false`, because "the underlay strategy only works when the primary plane supports blending" **[read: `overlay_strategy_underlay.{h,cc}`, `TryOverlay`, `AdjustOutputSurfaceOverlay`]**. The hole is `ReplaceExistingQuadWithSolidColor(quad, kTransparent, kSrcOver)` with `needs_blending = false`, i.e. a clear; or, for a translucent quad, black with `kDstOut` **[read: `components/viz/common/quads/render_pass_internal.cc`, `ReplaceExistingQuadWithHolePunch`]**. Single-on-top refuses a candidate occluded by quads above it **[read: `overlay_strategy_single_on_top.cc`]**.
- **macOS.** `OverlayProcessorMac` first tries to turn *every* quad of the root pass into a CALayer overlay; on success "to avoid drawing the root RenderPass, we set `damage_rect` to be empty" **[read: `overlay_processor_mac.cc`, `ProcessForOverlays`]**. Each quad, including video, is then its own `CALayer`, and content above the video is just a later layer **[read: `ca_layer_overlay.cc`, `ProcessForCALayerOverlays`]**. It gives up above a quad count (128 by default, 1024 with `kCALayerNewLimit`), above 30 render-pass quads, with copy requests or video capture **[read: same]**. Then the frame is drawn by the GPU into the primary plane, except that hardware-protected video, and HDR video under `CoreAnimationHDRUnderlays` ("so that we can use the tone mapping provided by macOS"), are forced into separate layers beneath it with a hole punched (`PutQuadInSeparateOverlay` → `ReplaceExistingQuadWithHolePunch`) **[read: same]**. Transparent solid-colour quads and invisible quads are skipped, never becoming layers **[read: `ca_layer_overlay.cc`, `FromSolidColorDrawQuad`, `FromDrawQuad`]**.

### GTK 4

- **The widget.** `GtkGraphicsOffload` "bypasses gsk rendering by passing the content of its child directly to the compositor", Wayland only; prevented by non-rectangular clips, unsupported dmabuf formats, translucent content, transforms beyond translation and scale, and filters such as opacity **[read: `gtk/gtkgraphicsoffload.c`, class doc]**. Its snapshot pushes a subsurface node around the child, with an optional black background **[read: `gtk_graphics_offload_snapshot`]**. A `GskSubsurfaceNode` "potentially diverts a part of the scene graph to a subsurface" **[read: `gsk/gsksubsurfacenode.c`]**.
- **The decision.** Before rendering, `gsk_offload_new` visits the tree. For each later node that intersects an offloadable texture or its background: if the node "clears the background", offload is disabled; otherwise the subsurface is "lowered because a … overlaps" (`can_raise = FALSE`) **[read: `gsk/gskoffload.c`, `visit_node`]**. Then it attaches the texture with `above = TRUE` if it can still be raised, else below **[read: same]**. Below, a translucent texture with no background is refused ("Non-opaque texture … below") **[read: `gdk/wayland/gdksubsurface-wayland.c`]**; the background is a `wp_single_pixel_buffer` subsurface placed below **[read: same]**.
- **The hole.** The GPU renderer, meeting a subsurface node whose subsurface holds a texture and is not above its parent, clears its bounds (a `clear_op` or a draw with `GSK_GPU_BLEND_CLEAR`); if it is above, it draws nothing there; if not offloaded, it draws the child **[read: `gsk/gpu/gskgpunodeprocessor.c`, `gsk_gpu_node_processor_add_subsurface_node`]**. The hole is the renderer's, from a typed node and the offload decision, never the widget's **[inferred]**.

### Android: `SurfaceView`, `TextureView`, Compose

- **`SurfaceView`.** "The surface is Z ordered so that it is behind the window holding its SurfaceView; the SurfaceView punches a hole in its window to allow its surface to be displayed. The view hierarchy will take care of correctly compositing with the Surface any siblings of the SurfaceView that would normally appear on top of it … it can have an impact on performance since a full alpha-blended composite will be performed each time the Surface changes" **[read: `core/java/android/view/SurfaceView.java`, class doc]**. `draw`/`dispatchDraw` call `clearSurfaceViewPort`, which calls `canvas.punchHole(…, cornerRadius, alpha)` **[read: same]**. `setZOrderOnTop(true)` puts the surface above the window, with no hole; `setZOrderMediaOverlay` puts it behind the window but above other behind surfaces **[read: same]**. The view also adds its rectangle to the window's transparent region (`gatherTransparentRegion`), which `ViewRootImpl` sends as `setTransparentRegionHint` **[read: same; `ViewRootImpl.java`]**.
- **The hole is a typed op.** `SkiaRecordingCanvas::punchHole` records a `HOLE_PUNCH_ANNOTATION` (radii and alpha) and a black `kDstOut` round rect, and sets `setHasHolePunches(true)` on the display list **[read: `libs/hwui/pipeline/skia/SkiaRecordingCanvas.cpp`]**. `RenderNode::prepareTreeImpl` ORs children's flags into `mHasHolePunches` **[read: `libs/hwui/RenderNode.cpp`]**. When a node is drawn through an offscreen layer, HWUI first replays its display list with a `kDstOut` transform canvas into the target "to make sure we still punch a hole … then draw the layer on top" **[read: `libs/hwui/pipeline/skia/RenderNodeDrawable.cpp`]**. That is the case PR #11 answers by "a layer … no hole shows through" and gives up on **[inferred]**.
- **`TextureView`.** It "does not create a separate window but behaves as a regular View", allowing translucency, rotation and clipping; its "contents must be copied, internally"; its table lists HDR as "Limited (on Android T+)" against `SurfaceView`'s "Full", and only `SurfaceView` "Renders DRM content"; "SurfaceView is recommended as a more general solution" **[read: `TextureView.java`, class doc]**.
- **Compose.** `AndroidExternalSurface` is "a separate layer positioned by default behind the window … composition is handled by the system compositor which can bypass the GPU", z-order `Behind`, `MediaOverlay` or `OnTop`, opaque by default; `AndroidEmbeddedExternalSurface` is composed "like any other UI widget, using the GPU", for when the surface must be "sandwiched" between widgets or take `graphicsLayer` effects **[read: androidx `compose/foundation/.../AndroidExternalSurface.android.kt`]**.

### Brief: Qt Quick, Compose Multiplatform desktop, WPF

- **Qt Quick.** An item draws only through scene-graph nodes, after setting `ItemHasContents` and implementing `updatePaintNode` **[read: https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qquickitem.html]**. `VideoOutput` renders frames through its `QVideoSink` **[read: https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qml-qtmultimedia-videooutput.html]**; that these become a texture node in the scene graph is **[inferred]**.
- **Compose Multiplatform desktop.** `SwingInteropViewHolder` draws `drawRect(Color.Transparent, blendMode = BlendMode.Clear)` "to make visible the component under our canvas" **[read: `compose/ui/ui/src/desktopMain/.../SwingInteropViewHolder.desktop.kt`]**. Components go below only when interop blending is on and supported (`shouldPlaceInteropAbove = !useInteropBlending || metalOrderHack`), and the Skia layer is made transparent for it **[read: `ComposeSceneMediator.desktop.kt`]**. The flag "allows drawing compose elements above interop", works "only with Metal, DirectX and offscreen rendering", and "on DirectX, it cannot overlay another DirectX component" **[read: `ComposeFeatureFlags.desktop.kt`]**.
- **WPF.** "Each pixel within the window belongs to exactly one HWND … Attempting to render WPF pixels over Win32 leads to undesirable results" **[read: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/desktop/wpf/advanced/technology-regions-overview]**.

### Patterns

Three designs, plus the airspace limit (WPF, Compose Multiplatform without blending) where the framework cannot draw over a native surface at all.

1. **A hole in one buffer, surfaces beneath (underlay).** Inset, Android `SurfaceView` (default), Chromium's underlay strategy and its macOS fallback for protected and HDR video, GTK when something overlaps, Compose Multiplatform with blending. One GPU buffer per frame however many surfaces, and anything may be drawn above a surface at no extra cost, but that buffer must be blended: the window or view is non-opaque, and the system blends it over the surfaces every time either changes (Android's doc says so) **[read / inferred]**. HDR and protected video work, since the system shows the surface and nothing samples it **[read: Chromium forces exactly those into underlays]**. A translucent surface beneath shows whatever is behind the window, so GTK refuses it without a background **[read]**. The frame is still drawn even when it holds only holes, unless something knows they are holes. The designs differ on who knows. **Android records the hole as a typed op and propagates it up the tree; Chromium and GTK create the hole themselves in the compositor from a typed video quad or subsurface node, and so never need to recognise one.** Inset and Compose Multiplatform paint a generic clear, which is why PR #11 has to recognise holes by shape, and must give up under layers, where HWUI instead re-punches **[read / inferred]**.
2. **Slices around surfaces, overlays above (each surface its own layer in an ordered list).** Flutter on every embedder, Chromium's full-CALayer mode on macOS, WebKit's compositing layers (video in another process as a `CALayerHost` layer **[read: `WebKit/WebProcess/GPU/media/cocoa/VideoLayerRemoteCocoa.mm`]**, and, judging by the compositing reason `IndirectCompositingReason::Overlap` and the `LayerOverlapMap` it is computed from **[read: `WebCore/rendering/RenderLayerCompositor.{h,cpp}`]**, anything painted later that overlaps a composited layer is composited itself **[inferred]**), GTK when nothing overlaps. No hole is drawn. The bottom slice can be opaque; slices above a surface must be transparent outside where they draw, which Flutter's macOS embedder bounds with paint-region sublayers **[read]**. Content above a surface costs an extra buffer and an overlap computation (r-tree regions, quad lists), and the number of layers needs a cap or merging (Chromium's 128/1024 limit, Flutter iOS unions rects, Android HC++ uses one overlay) **[read]**. **"Nothing to draw" falls out per slice for free:** Flutter's `DlOpSpy` drops an empty slice, Chromium sets the root pass's damage empty when every quad is a layer, GTK's offloaded node damages nothing **[read]**. HDR and protected video work as in the first design **[inferred]**.
3. **The surface drawn as a texture.** Flutter's `Texture` (default `video_player`, Virtual Display, TLHC), Android `TextureView` and Compose's `AndroidEmbeddedExternalSurface`, Qt Quick's `VideoOutput`, and the fallback of Chromium and GTK when a quad or node cannot be promoted. Arbitrary transforms, opacity, clips and content above are free, but the GPU composites every video frame into the window's buffer (Android: "must be copied"), protected content cannot be shown and HDR is limited **[read: `TextureView` doc]**, and the whole frame is drawn whenever the video changes, so "nothing to draw" never arises **[inferred]**.

Where the compositor rather than the widget decides (Chromium, GTK, Flutter's engine), the UI layer records a typed item ("a video quad", "a subsurface node", "a platform view at this point in paint order") and the compositor chooses hole, overlay or texture per frame, with fallbacks. Where the widget decides (Android `SurfaceView`, Inset, Compose Multiplatform), the hole is fixed when painting, and the z-order is the app's choice (`setZOrderOnTop`) **[inferred]**.

### Android

- `SurfaceControlViewHost` is a "utility class for adding a View hierarchy to a SurfaceControl … The primary usage of this class is to embed a View hierarchy from one process in to another" **[read: `core/java/android/view/SurfaceControlViewHost.java`]**. Its constructor builds a container `SurfaceControl` named `SurfaceControlViewHost`, a `WindowlessWindowManager` over it, and a `ViewRootImpl` with `WindowlessWindowLayout`, registered with `WindowManagerGlobal.addWindowlessRoot` **[read: same]**. `setView(view, width, height)` lays the view out "as if it were in a window of the passed in width and height" **[read]**. So the embedded side runs the ordinary view root; only the window manager under it is replaced **[inferred]**.
- `getSurfacePackage()` should be passed to `SurfaceView#setChildSurfacePackage`, "which will not only reparent the Surface, but ensure the accessibility hierarchies are linked" **[read]**. `setChildSurfacePackage` reparents the package's `SurfaceControl` in a transaction applied with the host's draw (`applyTransactionOnVriDraw`) **[read: `SurfaceView.java`]**. The embedded content therefore takes the host `SurfaceView`'s z-order: behind its window with a hole, or on top **[inferred]**.

### WebKit

- `LayerHostingContext::create` makes the context; on iOS with "a very large display ID to ensure that the context is never put on-screen without being explicitly parented"; on macOS `[CAContext setAllowsCGSConnections:NO]` then `remoteContextWithOptions`; with ExtensionKit and `useHostable`, a `BELayerHierarchy` instead **[read: `Source/WebKit/Platform/cocoa/LayerHostingContext.mm`]**. `createPlatformLayerForHostingContext(id)` is a `CALayerHost` with `contextId` set **[read: `WebCoreCALayerExtras.mm`, `_web_renderLayerWithContextID`]**. iOS hosting by handle goes through `createHostingHandle` and `BELayerHierarchyHostingTransactionCoordinator` with a `fence` **[read: `LayerHostingContext.h`]**.
- It is used for a whole page's layer tree (`TiledCoreAnimationDrawingArea::updateLayerHostingContext`), for video in the GPU process hosted in the web process's tree (`createVideoLayerRemote`, with `setVideoLayerSizeFenced` on resize), for WebRTC sample-buffer layers and for the model process **[read: `TiledCoreAnimationDrawingArea.mm`, `VideoLayerRemoteCocoa.mm`, `RemoteSampleBufferDisplayLayer.mm`, `ModelProcessModelPlayerProxy.mm`]**. Both `CAContext` and `CALayerHost` are private; WebKit and Chromium each declare them in their own SPI headers **[read: `WebCore/PAL/pal/spi/cocoa/QuartzCoreSPI.h`, Chromium `ui/base/cocoa/remote_layer_api.h`]**.

### Chromium

- **macOS.** `CALayerTreeCoordinator::EnsureCAContextAndRootLayer` makes an opaque, geometry-flipped root `CALayer` and a `CAContext` holding it; `CommitPresentedFrameToCA` commits the frame's layer tree into the root and sends `params.ca_context_id = [ca_context_ contextId]`, or an IOSurface Mach port when `allow_remote_layers_` is false; on a resize it drops the context and makes a new one with a fence port **[read: `ui/accelerated_widget_mac/ca_layer_tree_coordinator.mm`]**. The browser's `DisplayCALayerTree` keeps the fence ports and calls `GotCALayerFrame(ca_context_id)`, putting a `CALayerHost` under "a flipped layer, which acts as the parent of either `remote_layer_` or `io_surface_layer`" **[read: `display_ca_layer_tree.{h,mm}`]**. The GPU process never knows the window; the compositor's widget is a handle bound to one `NSView` at a time **[read: `accelerated_widget_mac.h`]**.
- **Windows.** The GPU process creates a hidden popup and a child window under it (`WS_EX_LAYERED`, `WS_EX_NOREDIRECTIONBITMAP`) **[read: `ui/gl/child_window_win.cc`]**; the browser reparents the child into its own window and moves it to the bottom, refusing a child not owned by the GPU process **[read: `ui/gfx/win/rendering_window_manager.cc`]**.

### Flutter

- `FlutterEngineAddView` takes a view id and its metrics and schedules a frame; the implicit view cannot be added this way **[read: `shell/platform/embedder/embedder.h`]**. With a `FlutterCompositor`, the engine asks the embedder for backing stores and hands each view's ordered layers to `present_view_callback` **[read: same]**. Without one, a Metal embedder supplies textures through `get_next_drawable_callback` and receives them in a present callback **[read: same, `FlutterMetalRendererConfig`]**. So a Flutter view is in no window unless the embedder puts it in one; the engine has no notion of a window **[inferred]**.

### Windows

- `DCompositionCreateSurfaceHandle` "creates a new composition surface object that can be bound to a Microsoft DirectX swap chain or swap buffer and associated with a visual"; `COMPOSITIONOBJECT_ALL_ACCESS` is always specified "except when duplicating a surface in another process, in which case set *desiredAccess* to 0" **[read: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/dcomp/nf-dcomp-dcompositioncreatesurfacehandle]**. That shares one surface's buffers, not a tree; Chromium's protected video uses it (`CreateSurfaceFromHandle`) **[read, prior: `2026-10-29-media-research.md`]**.
- WebView2's visual hosting goes the other way: "The RootVisualTarget is a visual in the hosting app's visual tree. … This visual is where the WebView will connect its visual tree"; it may be an `IDCompositionVisual` or a `Windows::UI::Composition::ContainerVisual`, or the sample's own `IDCompositionTarget` implementation **[read: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/webview2/reference/win32/icorewebview2compositioncontroller]**. How the browser process attaches its visuals across processes is not public **[inferred]**. WinUI hands WebView2 the `DCompTarget` of a `ContentExternalOutputLink`, whose `PlacementVisual` it puts in its own tree **[read: `controls/dev/WebView2/WebView2.cpp`]**.

### Patterns

1. **The producer exports a root, and the host imports it by a token.** Inset PR #12 with WaOS, Chromium on macOS, WebKit (`CAContext` id or `BELayerHierarchyHandle`), Android's `SurfacePackage`. The producer owns a whole layer tree and commits it on its own; the host places one layer and may put its own content above or below it. It costs no copy per frame, and the producer's tree may hold its own native surfaces, which is how WebKit's GPU-process video sits in a web page and how a WaOS program's video would sit in its picture **[read / inferred]**. Resizing atomically needs a fence between the two commits (`CAContext` fence ports, Android's `SurfaceSyncGroup`) **[read]**. On Apple's systems the API is private, except BrowserEngineKit's on iOS **[read]**. Android's package also carries input and accessibility, which the Apple contexts do not; WebKit and Chromium forward those themselves **[read / inferred]**.
2. **The host exports a target, and the producer attaches to it.** WebView2's `RootVisualTarget`, Chromium's child `HWND` reparented by the browser, and Flutter's embedder, which hands the engine textures or backing stores and composes them itself. The host keeps the tree and decides its commit; the producer needs a handle into the host's composition. With Flutter's shape the engine draws buffers and never owns a system layer, so native surfaces of its own become the embedder's platform-view layers **[read / inferred]**.
3. **A compositor whose root is a handle bound later.** Chromium's `AcceleratedWidgetMac` and Flutter's view ids: the compositor draws into something that becomes a window's content only when attached, which is what both patterns above build on **[read / inferred]**.
