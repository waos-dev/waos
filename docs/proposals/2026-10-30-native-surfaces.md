# Native surfaces in Inset's frames

**Decisions taken (2026-10-30).** The lead took these, one question at a time, reviewing Inset's pull requests #11 (no GPU device or drawing until a frame paints something of its own) and #12 (a surface root no view shows, for WaOS's window processes), and asking for the way similar frameworks settled it rather than a patch. The research is `2026-10-30-native-surfaces-research.md`. Media decision 3 (`2026-10-29-media.md`) had chosen a clear hole in the framework's picture with the surface beneath; it stands as what a host does today.

1. **The scene records each surface at its place in the paint order, and the host makes room for it**, as Chromium's video quads, GTK's `GskSubsurfaceNode` and Flutter's `addPlatformView` do: the surface view paints nothing, `SceneBuilder::add_surface` records a typed entry in the picture that draws nothing by itself, and the scene knows whether anything besides surfaces was painted. A host makes room at each entry, today by clearing it with the surface beneath; a frame that paints nothing beyond its surfaces moves the surfaces' layers and draws nothing. Not a typed hole the widget paints, as Android's `Canvas.punchHole`, which fixes the hole at paint time, so a host whose swap chain cannot be transparent (Windows' default) would have no other way; not Flutter's slices with overlay buffers above surfaces, which costs a buffer and an overlap computation for content above a surface and which a host can still choose later behind the same entry. Keeping what a thing is known to the host is the point: #11's guessing of holes by their shape goes.
2. **Inset's scene becomes Flutter's layer tree, and valo only draws** (2026-10-30): a frame's scene keeps its layers (pictures, transforms, clips, opacity and the rest, and each native surface as a layer of its own) instead of flattening them into one valo picture with the surfaces listed beside it, a choice made on 2026-09-12 before native surfaces existed. The host walks the tree: it places each surface, draws the pictures with valo making room at each surface, and draws nothing when nothing besides surfaces was painted, as Flutter's rasterizer walks its layer tree and asks its embedder for a canvas at each platform view (`PlatformViewLayer::Paint`, `CompositeEmbeddedView`). Not a typed entry in valo's display list, which would put a compositing concept into a drawing library whose lists, like Flutter's display lists, hold only drawing; not the scene builder punching and counting its own holes in the flattened picture, which builds further on the flattening and fixes the hole at recording. #12's root no view shows stands (Chromium's split: WaOS exports the root layer in its own `CAContext`).
3. **A host gets the tree itself, and turning it into slices is a utility of `inset-embedder`, not an interface** (2026-10-30): `View::present` receives the scene's layer tree, the rawest data there is, and `inset_embedder::slices(&scene)` turns it into the drawing between surfaces and each surface with the transforms, clips and opacity above it, which every host calls today; a host that someday needs a walk of its own, as Flutter's iOS overlays do, walks the tree itself. Flutter's walk calls an embedder at each platform view (`PrerollCompositeEmbeddedView`, `CompositeEmbeddedView`) because its embedders never see the layer tree; Inset's do, so no callback trait is needed until a host needs one.
4. **An opacity above a surface clears its hole by that opacity, and the surface stays at full opacity** (2026-10-30): the buffer keeps that share of what was painted beneath, and the surface shows through the rest, which is exact on an opaque window, as every Inset window is. Not Flutter's opacity on the surface's layer, right for Flutter's surfaces above a slice but, with Inset's surfaces beneath the buffer, blending the surface with the window's background instead of what was painted under it; not ignoring it, as today.
5. **A backdrop filter over a surface is a known gap** (2026-10-30): the filter reads the buffer, where the surface is a hole, so a frosted panel over a video blurs the framework's pixels and leaves the video sharp, as Flutter's embedder API (macOS, Windows, Linux) also shows it; revisited when an app needs glass over a surface. Not a backdrop-filter mutator passed to hosts: macOS has only `NSVisualEffectView`'s system materials publicly, and a real backdrop over a layer needs a private class. Colour and image filters and shader masks join with their layers, a surface under one showing unfiltered, as in Flutter.
6. **A program's surface placement crosses WaOS's WIT with what Flutter's embedder API carries** (2026-10-30): a transform, a rectangle clip, a rounded-rectangle clip and an opacity, a path clip falling back to its bounds; the program's hole is still cut by the exact path, so the bounds show the surface beyond the path only where the program painted nothing opaque around it. Not every mutator with a path in the WIT, which no program uses yet; `clip-path` can join in a later version of the WIT without changing the shape.
7. **One change for the layer tree and a GPU device made only when a frame draws**, the two being coupled (2026-10-30): #11's lazy device, renderer and window surface join the layer-tree work, whose `slices.paints()` says when a frame draws, and #11 with its guessing of holes by shape is closed. The work is left uncommitted in a worktree for the lead's review, as all work now is.
8. **Names** (2026-10-30): the tree's `Layer` (`Container(EngineLayer)`, `DisplayList(DisplayListLayer)`, `Surface(SurfaceLayer)`), `ContainerLayer` with its `Effect`, after flow's layers, with Inset's word "surface" for a platform view as `add_surface` and `SurfaceView` have it; the utility `slices(&scene) -> Slices` of `Part::Slice` (flow's `EmbedderViewSlice`, the drawing between surfaces) and `Part::Surface(SurfacePlacement)` with Flutter's `Mutator`s. Not Flutter's `PlatformViewLayer` and placement, which would differ from the rest of Inset's API.
9. **A view no window shows, in place of #12's detached root** (2026-10-31): on macOS an app can open an Inset view that is in no window, whose root layer the app places itself (WaOS in its `CAContext`) and whose size, scale and screen the app gives; Inset's standard flow runs for it as for any view (frames, the scene, `slices`, placing surfaces, drawing only what was painted), so WaOS's program canvas places a program's picture there as it does in WaOS's own windows. As WebKit's page renders in a drawing area whose root layer is in a `LayerHostingContext`, Android's `SurfaceControlViewHost` runs the ordinary view root over a windowless window manager, and Flutter's embedder views are ids with metrics; Inset's `View::window()` already answers none for a view in nothing the app may manage. Not a bare root whose surfaces the app or the surfaces themselves place, which ran none of the view's flow and needed a surface to refer to itself. Reached through `MacOs` for now, not through `request_view`.
10. **On macOS every view is its layers: a root layer, a Metal layer Inset makes in it for drawing, and the surfaces placed under it** (2026-10-31), a window and a view in no window differing only in where the root comes from (a window's view's layer, or a free layer the app is given); windows too draw into the Metal layer Inset makes rather than one wgpu makes from the window handle, as Flutter's macOS embedder and Chromium make and arrange their own layers in a view. Not the window's own drawing path kept beside the detached view's, which left drawing, placing and the registry with a window case and a detached case each. Other systems keep drawing through the window handle.
11. **No type for who holds a view** (2026-10-31): the three things that differ between a window's view and a view in no window (where its root layer comes from, where its metrics come from, what ends it) are each decided by the holder, which already knows its view: the window's code maps a window's events to its view, and the app's `DetachedView` handle names its view. The registry, drawing, placing and frames see only a view and its layers; `View::window()` answering none is the one public sign. The detached view's own requests become the ordinary ones a window's view goes through.
12. **One concept for native content: a layer, as Core Animation has it** (2026-10-31): a node in the system's layer tree that is placed, holds layers under it, and shows content, buffers presented to it or a layer the system draws itself. Inset's `Surface` and `SurfaceParent`, the compositor's `root(view)`, a view's root layer and the detached view's layer were one idea under several names, each named when it was added, and the host's own names (a view's output or layers, window surfaces, placed surfaces) followed; good names follow from this concept, with a view (what the framework draws into, its root a layer) and a window (what holds a view) beside it. Not Android's two concepts, a node and a content endpoint, which earn their keep where producers live in other processes; Inset's live in-process. The `ThisOs` naming cleanup in the host (Rust's `sys` convention: `os::System` and plain type names, the concern traits in `os::capabilities`) is agreed and comes after this change, as a change of its own.
13. **The native node keeps the name surface, with Wayland's meaning, and its children are subsurfaces** (2026-10-31): decision 12's concept, named after the system whose model it matches exactly (`wl_surface` with content and subsurfaces, a subsurface's position applied at its parent's commit, `wl_buffer.release`), and not "layer", which Inset's framework already uses for Flutter's layers (`ContainerLayer`, the scene's `Layer`).
14. **A surface is made on its own, and whoever places it adopts it** (2026-10-31), as Core Animation's `addSublayer` decides a layer's parent: a view's frame places surfaces from its scene, and a surface places its subsurfaces; one parent at a time, a second placer reported. No parent object, `open_child` or `root(view)`; on Wayland the host makes the subsurface link at the first placement.
15. **A surface's content is buffers presented to it or a layer the system draws itself, filling it** (2026-10-31), set by whoever produces into it, one replacing the other, as `CALayer.contents` against a layer that draws itself: `present(buffer, released)`, and on macOS `MacOs::show_system_layer(&surface, &layer)` for an `AVPlayerLayer` or a `CALayerHost`. Not a system-drawn layer wrapped as a surface of its own, which would be a surface that takes no presents.
16. **A parent places a subsurface, and the place lands at the parent's next present, with its new buffer** (2026-10-31), Wayland's synchronized subsurfaces: `canvas_surface.place(&video_surface, placement)` then `canvas_surface.present(buffer, released)`. Not one value carrying a surface's content and its children's positions (`SurfaceFrame`), which read as a picture presenting a frame that holds children; not a transaction over the whole tree, which would let one producer commit another's surfaces. `released` is Wayland's `wl_buffer.release`: the buffer may be drawn into again.
17. **"Picture" keeps only Flutter's meaning, a recorded drawing** (2026-10-31): WaOS's rendered `Picture` (an IOSurface) becomes `Buffer`, Inset's word for the platform's buffer, its `pictures` module `buffers`, and the surface showing a program's drawing its `canvas_surface`; done when WaOS moves to the new surface API.
18. **The native node is a `CompositionLayer`, and "surface" means only something that holds pixels** (2026-10-31), reversing decision 13's name: across the platforms a surface holds pixels (Core Animation's `IOSurface`, DirectComposition's surface, Android's `Surface`, wgpu's and Vulkan's surface) and the node is a layer, visual or surface control; Wayland's `wl_surface`, node and content at once, is the exception. The node is qualified as a composition layer, a layer of the system's compositor, so it is not taken for Flutter's framework layers. Decisions 12 to 17 keep their model under these words: a composition layer is made on its own and adopted by whoever places it, shows buffers presented to it or a layer the system draws, places its sublayers to land at its next present, and releases each buffer. Inside the host, what a view shows into is the view's layer (`ViewLayer`; on macOS `CoreAnimationViewLayer`), and wgpu's drawing target keeps the word surface.
19. **Each holder sizes a view's root, and the view only fits its drawing to it** (2026-10-31): AppKit sizes a window's view's layer with the window, and the app's handle sizes a free root from the metrics it gives, in the same Core Animation transaction as the view's resize; no flag tells the view's fit whether the root is its to size.
20. **No view in no window: the app places a composition layer in a `CALayer` of its own** (2026-11-01), reversing decisions 9, 10, 11 and 19: with a layer made on its own and placing its sublayers at its own present (decisions 14 and 16), WaOS's process needs no Inset view to show a program's picture. It makes the canvas layer, places it in its `CAContext`'s root with `MacOs::place_layer_in(&root, &canvas_layer, &placement, scale)`, as a view's frame places a layer in its window, and presents to it, as Chromium's GPU process puts its root `CALayer` in a `CAContext` with nothing view-like around it. Placing, not handing out the layer's own `CALayer`, keeps the layer's `CALayer`s (its clip, its content, its buffer) laid out from one placement whoever places it, and makes the app one more placer that adopts the layer. macOS windows keep drawing through the `CAMetalLayer` wgpu makes from the window handle; Inset making its own layers for a view comes back only when something needs it.
21. **The name stays `CompositionLayer`, not `HostLayer`** (2026-11-01): it pairs with what makes it, `Compositor::make_layer`, as Windows' `Windows.UI.Composition` does; "host" already means Core Animation's `CALayerHost`, which WaOS's window process shows a layer of next to it, and WaOS itself, the host of programs, whose programs' layers would read as WaOS's.
22. **The pixels a producer presents are a `HardwareBuffer`, and a player's or camera's picture a `VideoFrame`** (2026-11-01), amending decision 17: Inset's `Buffer` (an `IOSurface`) becomes `HardwareBuffer`, Android's name for one block of pixels the GPU, the compositor and other processes all reach, so it is not taken for WebGPU's buffer of plain GPU memory, which WaOS's programs have beside it; WaOS's rendered `Picture` becomes `HardwareBuffer` too, and its `pictures` module `hardware_buffers`. The media frame reader's `PixelBuffer` (a `CVPixelBuffer`) becomes `VideoFrame`, WebCodecs' name, so `PixelBuffer` keeps valo's one meaning, pixels in CPU memory. Whether a video frame should simply be a `HardwareBuffer`, so that a decoded frame goes straight to a layer, waits for a check of how CoreVideo's pools reuse a frame's `IOSurface`.
23. **A layer's children come with each of its presents: `present(buffer, children, released)`** (2026-11-01), reversing decision 16's split of placing from presenting: a present shows exactly the children it names, each with its placement, and lets go of the ones it no longer names, as a frame-based compositor takes them, Chromium's `SubmitCompositorFrame` naming the surfaces a frame embeds and Flutter's embedder handing its compositor each frame's layers. Both of Inset's placers make whole frames, a view's scene and WaOS replaying a program's frame, so the list passes straight through and the one comparison of a frame with the last stays in the host, which makes it for a view's scene already. Not `add_child`, which reads as a child that stays until removed, as `addSublayer` and `appendChild` do: that retained model, every system's own, would have WaOS, or the program's embedder, compare each frame's placements with the last to add and remove children. Not a separate `place` landing at the next present, which said none of this. A program's placements cross WaOS's WIT with the frame they belong to, in the call that presents it; the layers cross as resources, and a playing video sends no frames of the program's.
24. **The framework's names follow the composition layer** (2026-11-01), so "surface" means only pixels: the widget is `CompositionLayerView` (`RenderCompositionLayerView`), the node of the frame's tree that stands for one is an `EmbeddedLayer`, after Flutter's embedded views (`ExternalViewEmbedder`, `EmbeddedViewParams`), added by `SceneBuilder::add_embedded_layer`, the slices' pairs come from `Slices::composition_layers` (`Part::CompositionLayer`), and the media calls are `set_video_layer` and `set_preview_layer`. Not "surface" kept in the framework after Android's `SurfaceView`, which would mean pixels in the host and a placed layer in the framework; not Flutter's "platform view", a native view with input of its own.
25. **Pixel types stay the platform's own, and a conversion is the platform's own call, taking the type the platform's API takes** (2026-11-01), the lead's principle: nothing hides a pool, a copy or a lifetime for ergonomics, which both confuses an app and is hard to build where it does not follow the platform. `HardwareBuffer` stays the platform's buffer (an `IOSurface`) and `VideoFrame` the platform's frame (a `CVPixelBuffer`); no `HardwareBuffer` carries a frame inside it, and no import turns either into an `Image` the framework keeps. A CoreVideo pool hands a frame's surface out again once nothing holds the frame, whatever still reads the surface (measured), which an app holding images of frames would not see.
26. **The framework draws a video by holding its reader, and the host takes the newest frame as it draws** (2026-11-01), as Flutter's `TextureLayer` paints from the engine's registry, the web's `drawImage(video)` takes the element's current frame and WebGPU's imported video texture expires with its task, so a frame is held only while it is on screen: `VideoFrameView::new(reader)` → `RenderVideoFrameView` → `VideoFrameLayer` → `SceneBuilder::add_video_frames`, and `slices(&scene, frame_image)` takes each reader's image from the host. The names follow what they show and the platforms' convention (Android's `ImageReader` reads `Image`s, `TextureView` beside `SurfaceView`), with `FrameReader` becoming `VideoFrameReader` since a frame is also the framework's; not Flutter's `Texture`, shaped for a registry crossed by method channels.
27. **The host asks each reader a view shows at every vsync** (2026-11-01), as a display link asks AVFoundation's player output for each refresh's frame; Apple's player tells no frame as it comes, and Chrome draws the web's `requestVideoFrameCallback` from its own vsync pull. An optional notice that frames resume, Apple's `outputMediaDataWillChange`, lets the vsync rest while a video is paused, when that matters. Processing every frame, a stream with backpressure (WebCodecs, `AVAssetReader`, `ImageReader.acquireNextImage`), is a separate family of interfaces, for when an app needs it.
28. **Known gaps from the independent review of the interface** (2026-11-01), each for the step that needs it: GPU fences in a present and its release, which Android, Wayland and D3D12 carry and Core Animation takes none of (documented for Apple: wait until the GPU work is scheduled before presenting), designed with the second host; a producer's size and resizing in step with the view (Wayland's synchronized subsurfaces, Android's `SurfaceSyncGroup`), with WaOS's step; a frame's time, rotation and colour, and the decoder's own formats for HDR, with the frames a host converts itself; colour per layer and who allocates a layer's buffers (DirectComposition's swap chains and registered buffers, Linux's format feedback), with those hosts.
29. **A reader is asked for the frame of the moment the picture reaches the screen: `VideoFrameReader::frame_for(display_time)`** (2026-11-02), from a second independent review: the host passes the target time of the refresh the draw shows at, a macOS display link's `targetTimestamp`, and a player's reader asks AVFoundation for that moment of the video (`itemTimeForHostTime`), as Apple's display link pattern, the web's `requestVideoFrameCallback` and Windows' `OnVideoStreamTick` are built around; Android's decoder releases each frame at its render time itself. Asked for "now", every frame showed one refresh late, behind the sound. Only the frames-out path needs it: a player shown by the system on a layer is timed by the system.
30. **`VideoView` becomes `PlayerView`** (2026-11-02), Media3's name for a widget that takes a player, so it does not read as a twin of `VideoFrameView`, the opposite mechanism. The widgets follow Android's: `CompositionLayerView` and `VideoFrameView` as `SurfaceView` and `TextureView`, generic and named after what they take, and `PlayerView` and `CameraPreview` for one source each on the layer path. Media3's and CameraX's switch to the framework-drawn path can join those later; `VideoFrameView` stays, Flutter's `Texture` and Android's `TextureView`, for what a layer cannot take and for hosts with no compositor, the only safe form of drawing frames in the picture. `CompositionLayerView` says it carries pixels only: no input, focus or accessibility, so not a native view.
31. **Known gaps from the second review** (2026-11-02), for the step that needs each: a layer giving its producer its size and a frame callback of its own (`wl_surface.frame`, macOS 14's per-view display link), with the producer's size of decision 28; one reader per consumer, with `read_frames` and `frame_output` behaving alike; a frame borrowed as a buffer, and an `Image` of a frame for a still or a thumbnail; a host saying which placements it applies (Wayland's subsurfaces cannot turn); DOM elements as the web host's layers; embedding native views, with their input, focus and accessibility.
32. **The scene's node for a video is `Layer::Video(VideoLayer)`, added by `SceneBuilder::add_video`** (2026-11-02), renaming decision 26's `Layer::VideoFrames(VideoFrameLayer)` and `add_video_frames`, so the variant and the type it holds agree, as `Embedded(EmbeddedLayer)` does: Chromium's `cc::VideoLayer` is this node, over its frame provider, and "layer" alone is the framework's in Inset's words, a native one always a composition layer. The widget and the reader keep "frame" (`VideoFrameView`, `VideoFrameReader`), as they deal in frames. Which frame a video shows stays the source's to pick for the display time it is given (decision 29), the form every kind of source fits without a clock of its own: a live one answers its newest, a timed one its frame for that moment, as Chromium's `UpdateCurrentFrame(deadline)` does; not frames pushed with times for the host to pick from (Android's `BufferQueue`, `AVSampleBufferDisplayLayer`), which Apple's player could only feed through a display link of its own. Where pushing with a time is the platforms' model, a composition layer's present (Metal's `present(atTime:)`, `SurfaceControl.Transaction.setDesiredPresentTime`, `IPresentationManager::SetTargetTime`, Wayland's commit timing), "present at a time" is a known gap until a producer presents timed video on a layer.
33. **An app adds a view on a composition layer of its own, and controls it as Flutter's embedder controls a view** (2026-11-03), for WaOS's prompts shown in a program's app on macOS: `Platform::add_view(layer, metrics) -> Option<ViewId>`, `set_view_metrics(view, metrics)` and `remove_view(view)`, Flutter's `AddView`, `SendWindowMetricsEvent` and `RemoveView`, none where a host cannot draw a view into a layer; its pointer and keys go through the framework's own entry points (`GestureBinding::handle_pointer_data_packet`, `HardwareKeyboard::handle_key_data`), naming the view, as the embedder's `SendPointerEvent` feeds the engine, not through the host. Compositing platforms put a view's size and lifetime on the side that draws it and its input through the framework's ordinary path: Flutter's embedder so, and Android's `SurfaceControlViewHost` (`relayout`, `release`, with its input routed by the window manager). Not a second kind of view beside window views, which left the rasterizer, the registry and the frame scheduler asking which kind they held; not a handle type held for a view; not the layer's placement standing for the view's size, which only layout systems do (an `<iframe>`, Windows' `ContentSite`) and which a placement's transforms would turn into a resize each frame.
34. **Every macOS view draws into a composition layer of its own** (2026-11-03), bringing back decision 10, which decision 20 deferred until something needed it: a window's code does for its view the three things an app does for its own (adds it on a layer Inset places in the window, sets its metrics as the window resizes, removes it with the window), and the rasterizer makes every view's surface from the view's own `CAMetalLayer`, as Flutter's macOS embedder and Chromium arrange their own layers. Windows, Linux and iOS keep drawing through the window handle for now: one surface source per host, chosen in its `os` module, not per view.
35. **What a view asks of the system is one trait, `ViewHost`, which whoever hosts the view provides** (2026-11-03): its input methods (`text_input`, Inset's `TextInputHost`), its cursor (`activate_system_cursor`) and its focus requests (`request_focus_change`), later its accessibility tree, gathered from `View::text_input`, `Platform::mouse_cursor` and `Platform::request_view_focus_change`; a window's view gets the host's, over its `NSView`, and a view the app adds gets the one it passes, `add_view(layer, metrics, host: Rc<dyn ViewHost>)`, so the app says what its view does rather than leaving one that does nothing. Input methods need nothing more: Inset's text input is Flutter's, whose platform side keeps a copy of the editing state and caret, so a sheet in another process answers macOS's synchronous questions from its copy. The name is Chromium's (`RenderWidgetHostView`, the platform view hosting one widget's content, its input methods, cursor, focus and accessibility, across processes in its app shims) and Android's (`SurfaceControlViewHost`); WebKit's `PageClient` is the same piece. Not a struct of optional parts handed to `add_view`; not `ViewEmbedder`, since "embedder" names Inset's whole host interface; not `ViewController`, UIKit's larger thing.
36. **A view the app asks for is one type, `ViewRequest`, a window's or one on a layer, and adding a view is a request** (2026-11-04): `Platform::add_view` answers a `RequestId` and its view arrives carrying it, as a window's does and as Flutter's `FlutterEngineAddView` calls back once added, so the framework's windowing owner hands every requested view to whoever asked through one map, and `run_app` builds only the views nobody asked for, with no flag on a view saying where it came from. `ViewRequest` holds the request, the view from its arrival until it goes, the platform's refusal and listeners; `WindowController` holds one and adds only a window's close handling; the old argument struct of the same name folds into `request_view(config, initial_data)`. A view's being in sight is its holder's to say, `set_view_in_sight`, as a window's code says it for occlusion. Pointer reports may name any view; each device of a stream the app forwards takes a number no other stream uses (a random 64-bit one), since the converter keeps one state per device number and two streams with no order between them must not share one; a window's own events share one number per device because one queue orders them. Not `View::added_by_app()`, nor a second controller and map repeating the window's bookkeeping.
