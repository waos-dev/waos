# Cameras and media

**Decisions taken (2026-10-29).** The lead took these, one question at a time, starting step 7's last capability. Already settled (v1 interface decision 5 and its notes, `2026-10-17-waos-v1-interface.md`): a player (a file or a link) and a camera are sources, each sent to a surface, a system layer beneath the program's drawing for overlay planes, HDR and protected video, to a frame reader, pictures as GPU textures, or to both; pictures are RGBA textures, converted on the GPU; the camera asks at first use, WaOS shows which program uses it, and opens each camera once and shares it. Players and cameras are common app capabilities, as Flutter's official `video_player` and `camera` plugins are, so they are Inset's, and WaOS wraps them; Inset already has the destination, `Compositor::open_surface` with its `SurfaceView` widget, on Apple's systems only, whose buffers are IOSurfaces.

1. **Media covers Apple's systems this round, with both destinations**: players through `AVPlayer` and cameras through `AVCaptureSession`, producers into Inset's surfaces and frame readers, and WaOS's WIT and engine over them. That builds the whole shape on the one system where Inset's compositor exists; the others each need a compositor of Inset's first (DirectComposition on Windows, Wayland subsurfaces on Linux, `SurfaceControl` on Android, a `<video>` beneath the canvas on the web) and their media stacks, and need no change to the shape. A program's picture is itself a surface in WaOS's window, so Inset's compositor gains surfaces holding child surfaces beneath their own buffer, as v1 noted.
2. **A player and a camera follow the platforms' own model, not Flutter's plugins'** (2026-10-29): `video_player`'s controllers, its `initialize()` and its copied `value` come from the plugin's method channel; the platforms share a simpler model. A player is one object made from a source, loading by itself, which the app controls and whose state it observes, shown by a view that takes it (`AVPlayer` and `AVPlayerLayer(player:)` or SwiftUI's `VideoPlayer(player:)`, Media3's `ExoPlayer` and `PlayerView`, Windows' `MediaPlayer` and `MediaPlayerElement`, the web's `<video>` being both). A camera is a session on one device with outputs, each for one use (`AVCaptureSession` with its preview layer and its photo, video data and movie file outputs; CameraX's `Preview`, `ImageCapture`, `ImageAnalysis` and `VideoCapture`).

```rust
let player = Player::new(app, MediaSource::link("https://example.com/movie.mp4"));
player.play(app);
player.state(app);        // PlayerState { status, playing, position, duration, size, ended }, a Listenable
VideoView::new(player)

let session = CameraSession::new(app, &camera);   // asks at first use
let photos = session.photo_output();
let frames = session.frame_output();
let movies = session.movie_output();
session.start(app);
CameraPreview::new(session)
```

3. **The split, and how a video paints** (2026-10-29): Inset's framework holds `Player`, a `Listenable` over the host's player, and `VideoView`, laid out at the video's size; the host opens and controls the media and connects it to a destination; Inset's compositor places surfaces with each frame. `VideoView` leaves its rect clear in the framework's picture and places its surface in the frame's scene there; the host moves the surface's layer, beneath wgpu's `CAMetalLayer`, in the frame's transaction; AVFoundation feeds the layer itself, never through the framework or the GPU drawing; the system's compositor blends the two. `show_on(surface)` hangs an `AVPlayerLayer` in the surface's layer, the only way HDR and FairPlay-protected video show at all, since protected pictures cannot be read, and with no copy; `read_frames()` reads `AVPlayerItemVideoOutput`'s IOSurface-backed pixel buffers for the app's GPU. In WaOS the program opens a surface on its view, WaOS makes it a child of the surface showing the program's picture, and the program places it with each frame it presents. Whether the other systems fit this model is checked by research before they are built.
4. **A camera's outputs this round are preview, frames, photos and recording**, each the system's own output, as AVFoundation's `AVCapturePhotoOutput` (captured, processed and encoded by the system) and `AVCaptureMovieFileOutput` (encoded and written to a file by the system) and CameraX's `ImageCapture` and `VideoCapture` are, and the web's `ImageCapture.takePhoto()` and `MediaRecorder`; none needs an app to encode. A photo answers its encoded bytes; a recording writes to a path the app gives, for a WaOS program a path inside it. Encoding and decoding by the app itself, as WebCodecs offers the web, is a lower capability of its own, for later.
5. **"The system" is the standard media stack the OS or its desktop ships, loaded where present** (2026-10-29), as research found the model holding on Windows, Linux, Android and the web (`2026-10-29-media-research.md`). On Android it is Media3 and CameraX, Google's own player and camera for every new app, which ship as AndroidX libraries inside the app, so inset-cli gains a step that packages AndroidX libraries: the way Android's platform is reached from here on. On Linux it is GStreamer, loaded at run time, as Firefox loads FFmpeg, so a system without it answers none rather than failing to start. With them come three changes the research found: a player sends to one destination at a time, a surface or a frame reader, since Media Foundation and an Android decoder give one or the other; `show_on` takes one surface at a time, since a `<video>` element and an Android decoder feed one; and on the web the host writes a recording's encoded chunks to its path, since `MediaRecorder` hands back chunks rather than a file, which is writing bytes, not encoding.
6. **A video is clipped by the surface Inset owns** (2026-10-29): on macOS an Inset surface is a clip layer cut to what the frame's scene shows, and the `AVPlayerLayer` hanging in it is clipped by Core Animation with the rest; on Wayland, which has no layer clipping its children, each subsurface crops only its own buffer (`wp_viewporter`'s source and destination), so GStreamer's `waylandsink`, whose subsurface Inset could move but not crop, is not used: GStreamer decodes into dmabuf frames and Inset attaches each to a subsurface of its own, cropped to what shows, still with no copy and still eligible for the compositor's overlay planes (how Chromium and Firefox are understood to show video there, not yet checked in their source). Linux therefore keeps `show_on`, with no system-drawn video layer of its own to defer to. How it is built depends on Inset's compositor on each system.
7. **A player puts its pictures on a surface it is given, as Media3's `setVideoSurface` does** (2026-10-29): every system has two levels, a view taking a player (`AVPlayerLayer(player:)`, `PlayerView.player`, `MediaPlayerElement.SetMediaPlayer`, `<video src>`), which is Inset's framework (`VideoView::new(player)`), and a player decoding into a surface (`setVideoSurface`, GStreamer's overlay handle, WinRT's `GetSurface`), which is the host's and the WIT's, since a program's own framework builds its view on it. The call is `set-video-surface(option<surface>)`, none to stop showing, one destination at a time, and a camera session's `set-preview-surface` likewise; Inset's host trait follows. *This renames decision 3's `show_on`.* WaOS's WIT, following Inset's shape: a view opens surfaces beneath its picture (`open-surface`), each placed by `place`, which takes effect with the view's next present, the WIT's side of `SurfaceFrame`'s children; `get-players` with `players.open(url)`, a path inside the program or a link; `get-cameras` with `list` and `open-session`, asking at first use; a frame reader's frames as textures on the program's own GPU device, RGBA, converted on the GPU; a photo output answering encoded bytes; a movie output writing to a path inside the program.
8. **Each program gets a capture session of its own, and WaOS shares no camera itself** (2026-10-29): on macOS two capture sessions in one process use the same camera at once, each at the camera's full rate (measured: 85 frames each in 3 s, with no interruption), so v1's "opens each camera once and shares it" is the system's own doing there. iOS, said to allow one session per camera at a time, is checked when WaOS runs there. A program's video from a link passes its network rules before WaOS hands the URL to the system's player, which fetches it itself: the network switch and the domain rule, the host looked up first as permissions decision 14 does for a proxy, so the local-network switch applies too.
