# video-player

A video player a person uses, with a library of their videos, as Apple TV's and Infuse's are, written as an Waft app in Fluent's look (Waft's WinUI kit) over `wasdk-adapter-waft`: what the lead uses to find what does not feel right in what WaOS gives a program on macOS. It is apart from `examples/player`, a demo of the three ways to show a video.

- **The library.** The user adds folders through the system's folder dialog (File › Add Folder… ⇧⌘O, the pane's Add Folder…, or the empty library's button); they are kept in `/data/folders`, and WaOS keeps each pick among the program's files, so they are there again at the next run. In each folder, and in the folders inside it two levels down, the videos are found by their type (`mp4`, `m4v`, `mov`, `qt`, `3gp`, `3g2`), hidden files aside. The window shows them in a grid of tiles, as many columns as its width holds: each a thumbnail, the system's picture of it a tenth of the way in at the nearest keyframe, small, read through `wasdk:media/media-assets`, drawn as an image of its hardware buffer with no copy, and cropped to fill its tile, with a bar along its bottom for how far it was watched, its title, the media's own where its tags give one, else the file's name, and its length; a video with no length, as a live stream has none, shows neither a length nor a bar. A `NavigationView` chooses what the grid shows, All, Continue Watching (the videos left partway, the one left last first) or one folder, in a pane beside the grid while the window is wide and along its top while it is as narrow as a phone; its search box keeps the tiles whose title holds what is typed, in any case. A folder's header takes it out of the library (Remove Folder), leaving its files where they are; Open… and Open Location… are there too. The lengths and titles read are kept in `/cache/videos`, each with its file's size and the time it changed, and the thumbnails in `/cache/thumbnails`, each by its video's URL, its file's size and the time it changed, so a later run shows them at once without reading the video: a thumbnail is encoded by the system's hardware codecs (`wasdk:media/image-codecs`) straight from the picture's hardware buffer, the buffer of the frame the system read, with no copy through the program's memory, as HEIC where the system encodes it and PNG otherwise, and decoded back by them into a hardware buffer of the program's own, which holds no decoder's frame, drawn as an image as the picture is; where the system has image codecs but no hardware codecs, a thumbnail goes through the program's memory, its pixels read back, encoded, and decoded there, which the log says once. A video whose length or thumbnail is not kept is read, one after another from the top of the library. A tile's thumbnail is a still image, so a library at rest asks for no frames. A click on a tile plays the video.
- **Opening.** File › Open… (⌘O) asks for a video in the system's file dialog, a sheet on the window, offering only what Apple's players play; File › Open Location… (⌘L) asks for a link in Fluent's `ContentDialog`, whose field holds Apple's HLS example stream, `https://devstreaming-cdn.apple.com/videos/streaming/examples/img_bipbop_adv_example_fmp4/master.m3u8`. A file or link the user opens with the app plays too. The window's title is what plays: the media's own title where its tags give one, else the file's name or the link's last part.
- **Playing and going back.** What plays fills the window, under its controls; the controls' Library button, Esc and File › Close (⌘W) go back to the library. Esc is the page's own answer to the dismissal Esc asks for, so a dialog over the page answers it first.
- **Controls,** over the picture along its bottom, Fluent's subtle buttons on a dark see-through bar: back ten seconds, play or pause, forward ten seconds, the time played, the seek bar with what is loaded, the time left, mute and the volume's `Slider`, the rate (a `DropDownButton` with a `MenuFlyout` of 0.5× to 2×) and the subtitles and audio (one with a menu of each). The seek bar is drawn by the app, not Fluent's `Slider`, whose default style shows no loaded ranges and which tells a click from a drag by nothing. The time and the bar follow the player's `position()` at each frame while it plays; a seek under way shows its target, and a drag the place under the pointer. A click on the bar seeks there exactly; a drag seeks as it goes, with any tolerance. A click on the picture plays or pauses. The controls and the pointer hide three seconds after the pointer stops, unless the player is paused or the pointer is over the bar, and show again as it moves.
- **The player's state,** shown plainly over the picture: a `ProgressRing` while it loads (`Loading`) and while it waits for data (`Waiting for data`) or is held back by the system; nothing while the system still judges whether data comes fast enough, as Apple advises; an informational `InfoBar` on why the system paused it (another app took the sound, the sound's output went, the app went to the background), with a play button; a replay button at the end; and an error `InfoBar` with a failure's kind in the user's words and the system's own words beneath.
- **Subtitles and audio:** View › Subtitles lists Off and the media's text tracks, View › Audio its audio tracks where it has two or more, each the one in use checked, as the controls' menu does. Subtitles are drawn by Waft's `SubtitleView`, inside its `PlayerView`, in the user's caption style, which the framework follows through `MediaQuery`.
- **Menus and keys:** the platform's menu bar: File (Open…, Open Location…, Open Recent, Add Folder…, Close ⌘W, which goes back to the library); Edit, the platform's, for the fields; Playback: Play or Pause (Space), Forward 10 Seconds (→), Back 10 Seconds (←), Faster (⌘]), Slower (⌘[), Mute (⌥⌘↓); View: the platform's Enter Full Screen, Subtitles and Audio; Window, the platform's. The app answers the Playback keys itself, among its app-wide shortcuts, so a text field still types a space and moves its caret with the arrows; the menu items show the same keys.
- **Window:** the picture keeps its aspect inside the window, letterboxed on black, as large as the window lets it be; the window keeps no smaller than 480 by 270 (`window.size-limits()`); full screen is View › Enter Full Screen. The app is in Fluent's dark theme throughout, its dialogs and menus too.
- **Remembering:** the ten media opened last, with where each was left, are kept in `/data/recents`, listed in File › Open Recent, and each opens again where it was left once its player can seek there; a tile's bar and Continue Watching read where it was left from the same list, so a video opened before ten others since has its place forgotten. Where it was left is kept as it pauses, as another opens or it is closed, and as the app is hidden or ends; media that ended, or was left in its last five seconds, or is live, opens at its start.

Asking to close its window quits it. Where the system has no players, its window says so.

To run it, start the WaOS built from this workspace, then send the program to it:

```sh
cargo waft run -p waos-app      # builds WaOS.app, and opens it
waos run -p video-player   # builds the program, and runs it in that WaOS
```

The program reaches a link's host under the network switch and its rule of domains, which WaOS checks before it hands the link to the system's player: the defaults, the network on for any domain, let Apple's stream through; with the network off its player fails as refused, which the window says. It asks no question of its user.

- **Takes:** `wasdk-adapter-waft`; Waft's `waft-widgets` (the app, its window, its menu bar, `Player`, `PlayerView`, `SubtitleView`, `RawImage`, the grid and the widgets the controls are made of), `waft-winui` (the theme, `NavigationView`, the buttons, `DropDownButton` and `MenuFlyout`, `Slider`, `TextBox`, `ContentDialog`, `ProgressRing`, `InfoBar` and the Fluent icons' font), `waft-embedder` (a player's state, its tracks, the media assets, the image codecs, a frame or pixels imported as an image, the file dialogs' options and picks), `waft-foundation` (the app, its state as an `Entity`, and timers), `waft-scheduler` (the ticker the position is read at), `waft-rendering` (how the bars, the picture and the tiles are hit) and `waft-services` (the keys), from crates.io; `url`, which a source's name is read from; `wasdk`, for the entry.
- **Provides:** a component, `target/waos/debug/video-player.wasm`, from `waos build -p video-player`.
- **Used by:** the engine's tests (`crates/waos/tests/all/video_player.rs`), through `crates/waos/tests/programs`; whoever runs it in WaOS.
