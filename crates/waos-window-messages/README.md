# waos-window-messages

What WaOS and a program's window process say to each other, as values, and the Mach transport that carries them on macOS, where each running program's windows are an app of its own (decisions 1 and 2 of `docs/proposals/2026-10-29-macos-apps.md`). The messages are one-way: nothing one side sends is a question the other waits on, but for a resize, where a window process that sent a view's new size with a fence waits for WaOS's word that the program's picture at that size is shown (decision 55 of `docs/proposals/2026-10-17-the-waos-project.md`), or until WaOS asks it for something the program awaits the answer to, which ends the wait, WaOS letting the fence go as it asks.

- **Takes:** `waos`, for the program's records as wasmtime generates them from the WIT and the engine's own values beside them (a window's configuration, macOS's title bar among it, how a view stands, what a program is opened with, what a file dialog offers, a notification as the program built it), which the messages carry as they are; `waos-package`, for a program's id; `waft-embedder` with its `serde` feature, for the values Waft's host gives a view and takes from it, which a prompt's dialog carries as they are (the view's metrics, a pointer's report, a key, a focus change, a cursor); serde and postcard, for the bytes; Mach, on macOS.
- **Provides:** `ToWindow`, what WaOS sends a window process: `Welcome` or `Refused` to its hello, a window to open for a request with where WaOS keeps the view's layers for it to show (`HostedLayers`: on macOS, the id of the `CAContext` whose root holds them, which the window shows in a `CALayerHost`), the program's `ViewCall`s on a view (its cursor and focus, whether it handles Back, the style of the system's bars over it, its text input, its window, its size limits, its move from a press, maximizing and minimizing among it), a key's reply, the program's end, and a prompt of WaOS's to show in a dialog window of its own in the program's app (`ShowPrompt`, with its size and where WaOS keeps its layers), what WaOS's view of it asks of the dialog (`PromptCall`: a cursor, the keyboard's focus) and the prompt's end (`HidePrompt`), the program's menu bar, whole, for its app to show (`SetMenuBar`), a context menu to open at a point of a view, named by a `ContextMenuId` its answer names back (`ShowContextMenu`), the program's answer to the user's quitting its app (`QuitAnswered`), and a file dialog to open, a sheet on the window of a view or on its own, with what it offers (`FileDialog`: open a file, open files, open a folder, save a file, with the engine's options), named by a `FileDialogId` its answer names back (`OpenFileDialog`), a notification of the program's to post under its app's bundle, now or at a time, named by a `PostId` its answer names back (`PostNotification`), one to take away, by the program's own id (`RemoveNotification`), macOS's own question for the program's notifications to put, named by a `PermissionRequestId` its answer names back (`RequestNotificationPermission`), and macOS's permission for them to read again, as WaOS comes to the front, which the app tells as it tells every reading (`ReadNotificationPermission`), and that a view shows the program's picture at a size, in physical pixels: its first, and each a resize waits for (`PictureShown`); `FromWindow`, what a window process sends WaOS: its `Hello` (which program, why it was launched, the user's click on one of its notifications among the reasons, what it was opened with), a window opened, with whether it has size limits, whether it is maximized and what the system's settings say a double-click on a title bar does, or refused, a `ViewReport` of what happened to a view (the methods of the engine's `ProgramView` the shell reports through, as values, its presence with its window's occlusion apart from its being on screen, its window maximized or no longer and the system's setting for a double-click among them; a new size, on macOS with the fence of the window's transaction that commits it when the window waits in the resize for the program's picture at it), a key, a window gone, what the system opened the app with after its hello, what happened to a prompt's dialog (`PromptReport`: shown, its view's metrics changed, in sight or not, a pointer's report, a key, its focus, or closed otherwise than as WaOS asked), an item of the program's own chosen in its menu bar, by its id (`MenuBarChosen`), a context menu closed, with the item chosen or none (`ContextMenuClosed`), the user's quitting the app, from its menu, the Dock or by logging out (`QuitRequested`), and a file dialog closed, with the paths on the machine the user picked, none when the user cancelled, or why it answered nothing (`FileDialogClosed`), which WaOS mounts for the program as they are, whether the system has a notification posted or scheduled, or why not (`NotificationPosted`), what the user did to one of the program's notifications after the hello (`NotificationActivated`), whether macOS refuses the program its notifications as the app read it, WaOS's asking among the times it reads (`NotificationPermission`), and macOS's answer to its question (`NotificationPermissionAnswered`); `bundle`, what a program's app bundle tells its window process: the keys WaOS adds to its `Info.plist`, the entry its loader calls, and the argument WaOS launches it with; `encode` and `decode`; `bootstrap_name`, the name a WaOS is found by; `PROTOCOL`; and, in `os::macos`, the transport: `ReceiveRight` and `SendRight`, the ports, checked in and looked up by bootstrap name, made and carried; messages of bytes and ports, sent without waiting and received with the sender's `AuditToken`, or `NoSenders` once the other side is gone; a send right the system made, such as Core Animation's fence, taken over (`SendRight::adopt`) and lent by its name to the call that takes one (`SendRight::name`); and `uptime_now`, the clock a program's input is stamped on in both processes.
- **Used by:** `waos-program-window`, whose windows carry out `ViewCall`s; the WaOS app, which serves its programs' window processes; and `apps/waos-window`, the window process.

The messages carry the program's records themselves, a menu among them: serde's remote definitions of the types wasmtime generates (`src/records.rs`), which take no derive of serde's, name each field and variant, and are checked against the records when the crate compiles, so a record the WIT changes fails the build until its definition follows. A prompt's dialog carries Waft's own values, which derive serde's traits under Waft's `serde` feature: it is WaOS's own Waft view that the dialog's input goes to, so its reports are what Waft's host in the window process reported of the dialog's view, naming that view and each pointing device by the window process's own numbers, which WaOS turns into its own view and numbers of its own as it forwards them. A text a program edits and the character a key typed, a program's or a prompt's dialog's, are left out of what a message's `Debug` writes, since messages are traced and either may be a password; so is what the user typed in reply to a notification.

A message is postcard's bytes of a value, in a Mach message whose id is `PROTOCOL`: a receiver refuses a message of another protocol whole, so a window process and a WaOS of different builds never misread each other. A change to a message adds at the end, and anything else raises the number. The bytes are inline up to 64 KiB and out-of-line memory above it, which the kernel maps into the receiver; ports cross as port descriptors, each a send right moved to the receiver. A send never waits: the ports are made with room for 1024 messages, and a full queue or a port that is gone fails at once.

The rendezvous is Chrome's (`docs/proposals/2026-10-29-macos-app-shims-research.md`, §3): WaOS checks in `bootstrap_name(data folder)`, the SHA-256 of its data folder's path under WaOS's bundle id, as Chrome names the port of a user data folder; a window process looks the name up and sends its `Hello` with a send right to a port of its own; WaOS reads the sender's audit token from the trailer the kernel appended, checks the process's code, and answers `Welcome` with a send right to a port of the connection's. Each side asks its port to hear `NoSenders`, which arrives once the other side's process is gone. `bootstrap_check_in` and `bootstrap_look_up` are not deprecated in macOS 27's SDK, and a name no launchd plist declares can be checked in outside the App Sandbox (`../experiments/2026-10-29-present-over-mach`).

The transport is `src/os/macos`, the one place in the crate where `unsafe` is written; it is tested over pairs of ports in one process, and the rendezvous over a name checked in by the test.
