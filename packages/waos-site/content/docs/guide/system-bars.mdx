---
title: System bars
description: Keep clear of a phone's bars and keyboard, and say how the bars look over your page.
---

A phone draws its status bar and its navigation bar over a program's view, and its keyboard over the bottom of it. A view's metrics say where, in physical pixels: `padding`, what the bars or the hardware cover now, such as a status bar or a notch, `view-padding`, what they may cover at any time, and `view-insets`, what they cover completely, such as the keyboard. A page's background runs under the bars; what must be seen keeps clear of them. The program also says how the bars look over its page: their icons dark over a light page and light over a dark one, and whether the system may put a scrim behind each for contrast. The program may also hide the bars, as a game or a video does to fill the screen; a swipe from an edge brings them back. A view starts with the platform's style, which on Android follows the system's light or dark mode, and a field the program leaves out keeps what was said before.

## With wasdk

```rust
// at each ViewEvent::Metrics
let metrics = view.metrics();
let below_the_status_bar = metrics.padding.top;
let above_the_keyboard = metrics.view_insets.bottom;
// over a light page, in a game that fills the screen
if let Some(bars) = view.system_bars() {
    bars.set_overlay_style(OverlayStyle {
        status_bar_icons: Some(Brightness::Dark),
        navigation_bar_icons: Some(Brightness::Dark),
        status_bar_contrast_enforced: None,
        navigation_bar_contrast_enforced: None,
    });
    bars.hide(SystemBarsBehavior::ShowTransientBarsBySwipe);
}
```

## With Waft

Nothing to write for the style: Waft works it out after each frame from what its widgets put under each bar, an `AnnotatedRegion<SystemUiOverlayStyle>`, as Cupertino's navigation bar puts one, and says it on the view. The bars and the keyboard are `MediaQuery`'s `padding` and `view_insets`, as in Flutter, which `SafeArea` keeps clear of. In WinUI's kit a window's content goes on a `FluentPage`, which paints its background under the bars and keeps its content clear of them and of the keyboard; with `extends_content_into_system_bars(true)` its content runs under the bars too and keeps its own controls clear, as a `NavigationView` does. To hide them, call `SystemChrome::set_enabled_system_ui_mode`, as in Flutter.

```rust
waft_winui::FluentPage::new()
    .background(Brush::Solid(resources.solid_background_fill_color_base))
    .content(home)
```
> `FluentPage` is WinUI's; `waft`'s `Page` is the `Navigator`'s, so a bare `Page` beside `use waft::*` is that one.

## With GPUI

GPUI has no word for the bars' style, so a GPUI app says it once, as it does on iOS with `gpui_ios::set_status_bar_style`: its icons light over a dark app, dark over a light one. Every window shows the style said last. What the bars and the keyboard cover is GPUI's window insets, which GPUI leaves to the app to keep its content clear of.

```rust
use wasdk_adapter_gpui::{StatusBarContentStyle, set_status_bar_style};

set_status_bar_style(StatusBarContentStyle::Light);

// In a window's root: padding by what the system covers, none on a desktop.
let insets = window.insets().effective();
div().pt(insets.top).pb(insets.bottom).pl(insets.left).pr(insets.right)
```

## Where it's missing

A desktop draws no bars over a view: `system-bars` is none there, and a window fills its screen itself ([Windows](/docs/guide/windows)). iOS neither styles nor hides its bars yet. The bars' colours are not offered: Android ignores them for an app drawn under its bars.
